// prompting basics

Five skills that cover most of it

Good prompts read like good briefs to a smart colleague who's new to your project.

01 Be specific about the task

Say exactly what you want produced, not just the topic.

VagueTell me about photosynthesis.
SpecificExplain photosynthesis in 5 bullet points for a 7th-grade science class.

02 Give context

Share who it's for, why you need it, and what you already know.

No contextWrite an email about the delay.
With contextWrite an email to a long-time client explaining our shipment is 2 weeks late due to a supplier issue. Keep the relationship warm.

03 Show an example

One good example communicates style and format faster than a paragraph of rules.

DescribedWrite product names that sound friendly.
ShownSuggest 10 names for a budgeting app. I like names in the style of "Mint" or "Copilot": short, warm, one word.

04 Ask for a format

Tables, checklists, JSON, word limits: tell it the shape of the answer.

Any shapeCompare these three laptops.
ShapedCompare these three laptops in a table with columns for price, weight, battery life, and a one-line verdict.

05 Iterate like a conversation

The first answer is a draft. Point at what's off and ask for a revision: "shorter", "more formal", "you missed the budget constraint", "give me three alternatives". Ask it to check its own work: "What assumptions did you make? What might be wrong here?"
